### Overview

Under [Prof. Jitender Kumar](https://www.bits-pilani.ac.in/pilani/jitender-kumar/) at BITS Pilani, a fellow student and I studied numerical semigroups generated by three consecutive triangular numbers, focusing on their **isolated gaps**. A manuscript from this work is being finalized for submission.

A numerical semigroup is a set of non-negative integers that contains 0, is closed under addition, and misses only finitely many numbers (its *gaps*). We studied

$$S = \langle T_n, T_{n+1}, T_{n+2} \rangle, \qquad T_n = \tfrac{n(n+1)}{2},$$

where an *isolated gap* is a gap $$x$$ whose neighbors $$x-1$$ and $$x+1$$ both belong to $$S$$.

### Research Objectives

1. Understanding the known results for embedding dimension two, starting from Singh and Ram's work on isolated gaps (*Communications in Algebra*)
2. Studying the Frobenius number (the largest gap), its parity and the symmetry of semigroups generated by triangular numbers
3. Partitioning the set of isolated gaps and finding its cardinality
4. Turning computational observations into proofs and writing them up for publication

### Learning Outcomes

We computed first and proved second: generating many semigroups in GAP and Python, spotting patterns, then proving them, with weekly sessions spent on edge cases. For the count of isolated gaps, I developed a new, elementary proof building on an idea from an earlier paper, which my supervisor adopted into our manuscript.
