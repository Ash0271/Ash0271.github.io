Numerical Semigroups of Embedding Dimension Three

Pure mathematics research at BITS Pilani

Overview

Alongside my applied machine learning work, I wanted a foundation in theory and in writing rigorous proofs. Under Prof. Jitender Kumar at BITS Pilani, a fellow student and I studied numerical semigroups generated by three consecutive triangular numbers, focusing on their isolated gaps. A manuscript from this work is being finalized for submission.

A numerical semigroup is a set of non-negative integers that contains 0, is closed under addition, and misses only finitely many numbers (its gaps). We studied

\[S = \langle T_n, T_{n+1}, T_{n+2} \rangle, \qquad T_n = \tfrac{n(n+1)}{2},\]

where an isolated gap is a gap \(x\) whose neighbors \(x-1\) and \(x+1\) both belong to \(S\).

Project Details

Duration: Jan 2025 – Aug 2026
Role: Undergraduate Researcher
Advisor: Prof. Jitender Kumar, Associate Professor, Department of Mathematics, BITS Pilani
Collaborator: Nachiket Garg

Research Objectives

  1. Understanding the known results for embedding dimension two, starting from Singh and Ram’s work on isolated gaps (Communications in Algebra)
  2. Studying the Frobenius number (the largest gap), its parity and the symmetry of semigroups generated by triangular numbers
  3. Partitioning the set of isolated gaps and finding its cardinality
  4. Turning computational observations into proofs and writing them up for publication

Technologies used

  • GAP (NumericalSgps package)
  • Python
  • LaTeX (Overleaf)
The semigroup generated by $$T_4, T_5, T_6 = 10, 15, 21$$. Its isolated gaps are 64, 74, 79 and 89, and the largest gap (the Frobenius number, 89) is itself isolated.

Learning Outcomes

Our workflow was to compute first and prove second: we generated many semigroups in GAP and Python, looked for patterns in their gaps, stated conjectures, and then tried to prove them. Weekly sessions with my collaborator and supervisor went into checking edge cases, which is where most of our early arguments broke.

For one of our key results, the count of isolated gaps, I developed a new proof that builds on an idea from a paper we had read early in the project. My supervisor adopted it into our manuscript. This project taught me how much patience a proof takes, how to write mathematics that someone else can check, and that a good idea from one paper can open up a problem in another.
